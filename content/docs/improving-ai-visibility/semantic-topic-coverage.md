# Semantic Topic Coverage

Covering a topic means answering the cluster of questions buyers actually ask
about it — not publishing one page with the topic in the title.

---

## Why one page is rarely enough

A buyer question expands into several retrieval queries. A single page usually
answers one or two of them well and the rest in passing, so it is retrieved for
a fraction of the cluster.

Coverage means the cluster has answers somewhere on your site, each genuinely
answered rather than mentioned.

---

## Finding the cluster

Use the [query fanouts](../core-concepts/query-fanouts.html) for the topic.
They are the engine's own expansion of the question, which is a better map of
the cluster than an internal guess.

Group them by intent. Fanouts about price belong together; fanouts about
integration belong together. Each group is a candidate piece.

---

## Depth before breadth

A common failure is a page per fanout. Thin pages give engines nothing to lift,
compete with each other, and signal low quality.

One substantial piece answering a coherent group beats five pages that each
half-answer one of them.

---

## Knowing when a topic is covered

Not when you have published, but when the conversations show you appearing
across the topic's scenarios rather than one of them. See
[Scenario Performance](../dashboards/scenario-performance.html).

Uneven scenario results under a covered-looking topic is the clearest sign that
coverage is nominal rather than real.
