# Structuring Content for LLMs

Answer engines quote passages. The single most common reason a good page is
never cited is that no paragraph in it answers anything on its own.

---

## The test

Copy any paragraph out of the page and read it alone. Does it answer a question
without the paragraph before it?

If yes, it is quotable. If it depends on setup, a pronoun or a heading to make
sense, an engine cannot use it.

Most marketing pages fail this everywhere.

---

## What makes a passage quotable

**It answers in the first sentence.** The conclusion first, the reasoning
after. Building to a point costs you the quote.

**It is self-contained.** Name the subject instead of "it" or "this". A
passage carried into an answer loses its context.

**It is specific.** Numbers, conditions and limits. "Pricing starts at $X per
user for teams under ten" is quotable; "flexible pricing for teams of all
sizes" is not.

**It uses the words of the question.** See
[Query Fanouts](../core-concepts/query-fanouts.html).

---

## Structure that helps

Headings that are questions. Short paragraphs, one claim each. Tables for
comparisons, because a table row is a self-contained fact. Explicit statements
of what something is *not* — those get quoted often, since they resolve a
distinction the asker was weighing.

---

## Structure that hurts

Walls of text with the answer buried. Information carried only in an image, a
PDF or a video without a transcript. Key facts spread across three sections so
none is complete. A conclusion that merely gestures at what came before.

---

## Where to start

Do not rewrite the site. Take the pages you already have for questions you
already appear in, and make their key paragraphs stand alone. That is the
cheapest visibility work available, because the retrieval already works — only
the quoting fails.
