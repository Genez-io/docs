# Monitoring AI Visibility

Visibility work has a long and noisy feedback loop. Monitoring well is mostly
about not drawing conclusions too early.

---

## A workable rhythm

**Weekly runs** on a stable set of topics. Often enough to catch a real change,
rare enough to be signal. See
[How often should I run conversations?](../faq/how-often-should-i-run-conversations.html).

**Keep the question set fixed** between runs. Changing scenarios while watching
a trend means measuring two different things.

**Record what you changed, and when.** Almost every "why did this move"
question is answered by a note somebody did not write.

---

## Reading movement

Before investigating a change, check whether competitors moved the same way at
the same time. If the whole category shifted, the engine changed, not you.
This one check prevents most wasted investigation.

Then: is it across all topics or some? Confined changes are yours; broad ones
usually are not.

Then: open the conversations from before and after and compare what was cited.
A changed answer almost always means a changed source.

---

## What to report

Direction and cause, not the number. "Visibility rose nine points on pricing
topics after the comparison page was published, and engines now cite it" is a
result. "Visibility is 62%" invites a question nobody can answer.

---

## Measuring what you actually care about

Platform metrics describe the medium. If you need to track something specific
to your business — being recommended for a particular phrase, in one market,
against two named competitors — that is what the
[Business Scorecard](../scorecard/index.html) is for.
