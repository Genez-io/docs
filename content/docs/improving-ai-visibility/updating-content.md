# Updating Content

Existing pages are usually a better investment than new ones, because
retrieval already works for them. Only the quoting, the accuracy or the
completeness is failing — and those are edits.

---

## What to update first

**Pages for questions where you appear but are not recommended.** The engine
already finds you and passes you over. The answers usually say why.

**Pages that are retrieved but never quoted.** Apply the test from
[Structuring Content for LLMs](structuring-content-for-llms.html): if no
paragraph stands alone, that is the fix.

**Pages contradicted by newer sources.** If your page and a cited third party
disagree about your own product, engines hedge or follow the third party.

**Anything factually stale.** Old pricing, removed features, superseded
integrations. Stale facts propagate into answers and are quietly expensive.

---

## What updating does not mean

Changing the date and republishing. Engines are reading the text; a touched
timestamp changes nothing.

---

## After an update

Note the date and keep running the topic. Expect a lag: engines have to
re-retrieve the page and decide it is better than what they used before. One
run is not a verdict.

See [Monitoring AI Visibility](monitoring-ai-visibility.html).
