# Backlinks and Citations

They are not the same thing, and confusing them wastes money.

---

## The difference

A **backlink** is an HTML link to your page. It matters for search engines,
which treat links as votes.

A **citation** is an answer engine using a source to support a claim. It does
not require a link at all — a page that describes your product accurately can
be cited without linking to you, and a page that links to you can be ignored.

Engines are reading the text. A link is not a vote to them; it is markup.

---

## What this changes

**A mention without a link still counts.** Being described accurately in a
cited source does the work, linked or not. Outreach that insists on a link
sometimes trades away the mention.

**A link from somewhere never retrieved does nothing.** Directory links and
low-quality placements built for search rankings are invisible here.

**Being described wrongly is worse than not being described.** A cited source
that gets your pricing or your category wrong propagates that error into
answers. Correcting it is real visibility work.

---

## Where the two overlap

A genuinely good page on a site buyers read will usually earn both. The overlap
is large enough that good work serves both purposes — the mistake is doing
link-shaped work that serves only one, and often neither.

---

## What to do

Find the sources already cited in your category. Check what they say about you.
Correct what is wrong, fill what is missing, and earn a place where you are
absent. Whether a link comes with it is secondary.
