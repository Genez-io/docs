# Understanding Your First Results

Your first run gives you a score. The score is the least useful thing in it.

---

## Read answers before you read numbers

Open five or six conversations and read them. Within ten minutes you will know
more than the dashboard can tell you: how engines describe your category, what
they cite, which competitors they treat as serious, and how your brand is
characterised when it comes up.

Teams that skip this step spend the next month interpreting a number they have
no feel for.

---

## Then look at three things

**Where you are absent.** Topics scoring zero are the clearest finding. Engines
answer that question without you, and the conversations show who they answer it
with.

**Where you appear but are not recommended.** You come up and get passed over.
That is a positioning problem, and the answers usually say why in plain words.

**What is cited.** The sources doing the work in your category. This is the
most actionable list in the product — see
[Most Cited Sources](../insights/most-cited-sources.html).

---

## What not to conclude yet

**Do not benchmark the headline score.** 40% means nothing until you see where
your competitors sit on the same questions.

**Do not treat one run as a trend.** Answer engines vary between runs. You need
several before a direction is real. See
[Why do LLM results change?](../faq/why-do-llm-results-change.html).

**Do not fix everything.** The first run usually surfaces more gaps than any
team can address. Pick the topics with real commercial weight and ignore the
rest for now.

---

## A reasonable first action

Take one topic where a competitor wins, read what the engine cited, and decide
whether you can be a better source for that question. That is one week of work
with a measurable outcome, which beats a strategy document.
