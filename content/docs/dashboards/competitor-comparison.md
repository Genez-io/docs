# Competitor Comparison

Every metric you have for your brand, measured the same way, for the brands you
compete with.

---

## Reading the comparison

Put the three numbers side by side and the shape of a competitor becomes clear:

- **High visibility, high recommendation.** The brand to beat. Engines both know
  them and endorse them.
- **High visibility, low recommendation.** Well known, not preferred. Often the
  incumbent everyone names and nobody picks.
- **Low visibility, high recommendation.** Dangerous. Rarely surfaced, but
  convincing when it is. A source problem away from being a real threat.
- **Low on both.** Not currently competing in AI answers, whatever they do
  elsewhere.

Your own position in that grid tells you which problem you have.

---

## Comparing fairly

The comparison only holds because every brand is run through the same topics,
the same scenarios and the same engines. A competitor's number here is not
their global standing — it is their standing on *your* question set.

That is a feature. It answers "who wins the questions my buyers ask", which is
the question that matters, rather than "who is bigger".

---

## When a competitor you do not recognise appears

Take it seriously before dismissing it. Answer engines surface brands by how
well sources support them, not by market share. A brand you have never lost a
deal to can still be the one an engine recommends — and that is a problem that
starts in AI answers and arrives in your pipeline later.
