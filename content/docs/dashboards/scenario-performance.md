# Scenario Performance

A topic is the subject. A scenario is one realistic way somebody asks about it.
Scenario performance shows which phrasings you win and which you lose.

---

## Why the distinction matters

Two scenarios under one topic can give opposite results. "Best CRM for a small
team" and "CRM for a team of five that outgrew spreadsheets" are the same topic
and often different answers, because they pull on different sources.

A topic score averages that away. The scenario view keeps it.

---

## What it tells you

**Which framing you lose.** If you win the general question and lose every
specific one, your content is positioning material with nothing concrete under
it. If the reverse, you have the detail and no overview.

**Where a competitor is specifically strong.** A competitor winning one
scenario consistently is usually being carried by one source. Open the
conversation and look at what is cited.

**Whether a scenario is realistic.** A scenario nobody would actually type
produces answers nobody would actually get. Scenario performance is where you
notice a badly written scenario, and rewriting it is often the fix.

---

## Acting on it

Scenarios are the level at which content briefs make sense. A brief aimed at a
scenario has a question to answer; a brief aimed at a topic has a subject to
cover, which is much weaker.
