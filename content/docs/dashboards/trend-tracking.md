# Trend Tracking

Single readings mislead. Answer engines vary between runs, so the value of
measuring repeatedly is the direction, not the point.

---

## What to trust

**A sustained direction over several runs** is a finding.

**A single jump** is usually noise, unless something specific changed — you
published, a competitor launched, or an engine updated.

A useful habit: before investigating a move, check whether competitors moved
the same way at the same time. If everyone shifted, the engine changed, not
you.

---

## Giving a change a cause

A trend line tells you when, not why. The sequence that answers why:

1. Note when the change began.
2. Look at whether it is across all topics or confined to some.
3. Open the conversations from before and after.
4. Compare what was cited. A changed answer almost always means a changed
   source.

Most unexplained movement resolves at step four.

---

## Reporting it

Report the direction and the cause, not the number. "Visibility is 62%" invites
a question nobody can answer. "Visibility rose nine points on pricing topics
after the comparison page was published, and the engines now cite it" is a
result.

Where you need the number to mean something specific to your business rather
than to the platform, that is what the
[Business Scorecard](../scorecard/index.html) is for.
