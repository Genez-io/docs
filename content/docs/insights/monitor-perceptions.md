# Monitor Perceptions

A perception is a claim answer engines make about your brand — "expensive",
"best for small teams", "hard to set up". The
[AI Perception Summary](ai-perception-summary.html) shows you what they are
saying now.

Monitoring turns that into something you can manage over time. Instead of
reading the summary and hoping a claim fades, you put the claim under watch and
Genezio keeps checking it.

---

## Why watch a perception

Some claims matter far more than their frequency suggests.

A single answer calling you "hard to integrate" can cost more than ten calling
you "reliable", because it lands exactly where a buyer hesitates. Watching it
tells you whether the work you did — the documentation you rewrote, the
comparison page you published — actually changed what the engines say.

Equally, a claim you *want* to be true is worth watching. If you are trying to
be known as the option for regulated industries, you want to know when that
starts appearing on its own.

---

## How to use it

1. Open **Perceptions** for your brand.
2. Track the claim you care about.
3. Come back after your next content push, or hit **Refresh** to check again
   now.

Each check is kept, so a tracked perception builds a history: what was being
said, and when it changed.

---

## Reading the result

A tracked perception gives you a verdict rather than a vague impression — a
straight answer on whether the claim is currently supported by what the engines
say about you.

That makes two things possible that a summary alone does not:

- **Prove the work landed.** "We rewrote onboarding in March, and the
  'hard to set up' claim stopped appearing in April" is a sentence you can put
  in front of a board.
- **Catch a reversal early.** A claim you fixed can come back, usually because
  a new source started being cited. Watching it means you find out in weeks
  rather than at the next quarterly review.

---

## Reading them from another tool

Tracked perceptions and their verdicts are available over
[MCP](../mcp/index.html) and through the API, so a perception check can sit
inside a wider report rather than being something somebody remembers to open.
