# Sentiment Analysis

Appearing in an answer is not automatically good. Sentiment tells you *how* your
brand is described when it comes up.

---

## What is measured

Every mention of your brand is read and classified as positive, neutral or
negative, and the balance is tracked over time.

Most mentions are neutral — an engine listing options is not praising them. The
signal is in the ratio and in which direction it moves, not in any single
answer.

---

## Reading it

**Neutral is normal, not a failure.** A category list is neutral by nature. Do
not chase a positive-only profile; it is not what a factual answer looks like.

**Negative mentions matter more than their share suggests.** One answer calling
you hard to set up lands exactly where a buyer hesitates. It can outweigh ten
neutral listings.

**Watch the trend, not the reading.** Sentiment moves as sources change. A step
down usually means a new source started being cited — find it in
[Most Cited Sources](most-cited-sources.html).

---

## From sentiment to a specific claim

Sentiment gives you the temperature. To act, you need the claim.

That is what [Perceptions](../core-concepts/perceptions.html) are for: the
specific things engines say about you — "expensive", "best for small teams",
"hard to integrate". A perception can be tracked over time and a verdict given
on whether it still holds. See
[Monitor Perceptions](monitor-perceptions.html).

The useful sequence is: sentiment tells you something changed, perceptions tell
you what, citations tell you where it came from.
