# Most Cited Sources

When an answer engine makes a claim about your category, it leans on sources.
This is the list of which ones, ranked by how often they appear.

---

## Why it is the most actionable report

Visibility tells you *that* you are losing. Sources tell you *where the answer
came from* — and a source is something you can act on.

A review site cited forty times in your category is forty opportunities. If
your profile there is thin, out of date, or missing, that is a concrete,
finite task with a known payoff.

---

## What to look for

**Sources you can influence directly.** Your own site, your documentation, your
profiles on directories and review platforms. If engines cite your competitor's
pricing page and not yours, that is a page to fix.

**Sources you can earn.** Industry publications, comparison sites, community
threads. Slower, but these carry more weight than your own site, because an
engine treats an independent source as better evidence.

**Sources you cannot touch.** Some citations come from places no outreach will
change. Knowing which is useful on its own: it stops effort going somewhere it
cannot work.

---

## Going deeper than the domain

A domain is often too coarse to act on. "YouTube was cited" tells you nothing
you can do.

Where Genezio can identify who published the cited material, it resolves the
citation down to the channel or the author — the specific YouTube channel, the
specific subreddit, the named writer. See
[Channels and Authors](channels-and-authors.html).

---

## A note on your own site

Being cited from your own site is good but limited. Engines weigh independent
sources more heavily, because a brand describing itself is not evidence. A
citation profile made up only of your own pages is fragile: one competitor
earning third-party coverage can displace it.
