# Genezio over MCP

MCP lets an AI client — Claude, or another tool that speaks the protocol —
work with your Genezio data directly. You ask in the client, and the client
reads your brand and acts on it.

It suits the work that sits between "open the dashboard" and "write a script":

> Which competitors gained ground on our pricing topics this month?

> Add a goal for being recommended on "CRM for agencies" in the UK.

> Fix the schema markup on the article we published yesterday.

---

## What it is good for

**Investigating.** Ask a question about your brand and get the answer from your
own data rather than from the model's general knowledge.

**Setting things up by describing them.** Create a scorecard goal by saying
what you want, without opening the board.

**Small corrections.** Edit an article's schema markup, check a perception, and
carry on with what you were doing.

**Work that mixes Genezio with everything else.** The point of an MCP client is
that it reaches your other tools too, so a single request can read Genezio and
act somewhere else.

---

## What it can reach

- **The Business Scorecard** — read the goals on a board, and create or change
  them. See [Scorecards over MCP](scorecards-over-mcp.html).
- **Perceptions** — the verdicts Genezio has stored about how your brand is
  described.
- **Content Hub** — the schema.org markup of an article.
- Your brands, topics, competitors and the conversations behind your numbers.

---

## Access

An MCP client reaches your account with your own permissions. It sees what you
see, and nothing a person in your seat could not open themselves.

Changes made through an MCP client reach the audit log like any other change,
so the record of who changed what stays complete.
