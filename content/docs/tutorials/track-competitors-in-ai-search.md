# Track Competitors in AI Search

How to use Genezio to watch a competitor properly rather than anxiously.

---

## 1. Let the answers name them

Genezio extracts competitors from the answers themselves, so start by seeing
who engines actually put beside you. See
[Extracting Competitors](../analysis/extracting-competitors.html).

Expect a surprise. The brands an engine treats as alternatives are not always
the ones you lose deals to — and a brand winning in AI answers today tends to
appear in your pipeline later.

## 2. Clean the list

Remove adjacent categories, parent companies and name collisions. They distort
[Share of Voice](../dashboards/share-of-voice.html), which divides the
conversation among whoever is listed.

## 3. Read the grid, not the ranking

In [Competitor Comparison](../dashboards/competitor-comparison.html), the
useful signal is the combination:

- high visibility, low recommendation — known, not preferred
- low visibility, high recommendation — dangerous; convincing when surfaced
- high on both — the brand to beat

## 4. Find where they win and you are absent

Topics where a competitor is visible and you are not. Each is a question your
buyers ask that you have no presence in — the most actionable list you will get.

## 5. Work out *why* they win

Open the conversations and read what was cited. A competitor recommended across
thirty answers is often carried by one or two sources. That is a specific thing
to respond to, not a general disadvantage.

## 6. Watch for changes, not levels

A competitor's absolute score matters less than its direction. Set the
comparison as a [Business Scorecard](../scorecard/index.html) goal if it is one
you review regularly.

---

## What not to do

Do not chase every competitor move. Answer engines vary run to run, and a
single shift is usually noise. Act on sustained direction with a visible cause
in the citations.
