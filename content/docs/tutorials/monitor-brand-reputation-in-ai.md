# Monitor Brand Reputation in AI

Reputation here is not sentiment in general — it is the specific things answer
engines say about you, and whether they are true.

---

## 1. Find out what is actually being said

Start with [AI Perception Summary](../insights/ai-perception-summary.html):
the claims engines make about you. Expect a mix of flattering, neutral and
wrong.

## 2. Separate the three kinds

**True and unhelpful** — "enterprise-focused" when you want mid-market. A
positioning problem.

**False** — a feature you do not lack, a price that is out of date. A sourcing
problem: something cited is wrong.

**True and damaging** — the honest weakness. Content will not fix this; the
product might.

Knowing which you have matters, because the response differs completely.

## 3. Put the important ones under watch

[Monitor Perceptions](../insights/monitor-perceptions.html) for the claims that
matter. A tracked perception builds a history, so you can prove a change landed
and catch a reversal in weeks rather than at the next review.

## 4. Trace a false claim to its source

Open the conversations behind it and read what was cited. A false claim is
almost always a cited source carrying an error. Correcting that page is the
fix — not publishing a rebuttal on your own site, which engines weight less.

## 5. Watch sentiment for the early warning

[Sentiment](../insights/sentiment-analysis.html) tells you the temperature, not
the claim. A step down usually means a new source entered the picture — find it
in [Most Cited Sources](../insights/most-cited-sources.html).

## 6. Make it routine

Weekly runs, a handful of tracked perceptions, and a note of what you changed
and when. Reputation damage in AI answers builds quietly, because nobody
receives a notification when an engine starts describing you differently.
