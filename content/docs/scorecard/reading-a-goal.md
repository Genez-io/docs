# Reading a Goal

Each goal is a card on your board. The card leads with where the goal stands
today — not with how it is configured.

---

## The card

A card shows the current state of the goal and its direction. Cards are drawn
in the shape that suits the goal: a trend over days, a comparison across answer
engines, a share between brands, or a grid. The shape is chosen for the goal,
so a share-of-voice goal does not arrive as a line chart that hides the split.

Where a goal concerns a competitor or an engine, the card carries its logo, so
you can read the board at a glance. Where a row names one of your products, it
shows the product's photo.

---

## Seeing where a number came from

Open a card and you can follow the number back to the conversations it was
built from. This matters in two moments:

- When a goal moves sharply and you need to know whether something real
  happened or the measurement shifted.
- When you present the number to somebody who will ask where it comes from.

Nothing on a scorecard is a figure you have to take on trust.

---

## Every day, automatically

Each goal is measured once a day and keeps yesterday's reading. You get a
history without running anything, and a goal created today starts building its
trend immediately.

---

## When a goal spans languages or markets

A goal can be set to a language, so a brand working across several markets can
keep one goal per market rather than one blurred average.
