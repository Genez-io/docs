# Understanding Conversation Results

A conversation is one question put to one answer engine, with the answer it
gave. Every number in Genezio is built from these, so this is the level where
you find out *why* a score is what it is.

---

## What a result holds

**The question.** The scenario as it was actually asked.

**The answer.** The full response, as the engine produced it.

**What was detected in it.** Whether your brand appeared, whether it was
recommended, which competitors were named, and which sources were cited.

---

## Reading one properly

Read the answer before the detections. The classification tells you what
happened; the prose tells you why — and the why is usually obvious once you
read it.

Three questions worth asking of any answer:

- **Where did this come from?** The citations are the material that produced
  it. If a competitor is recommended on the strength of one review, that review
  is the thing to address.
- **How is my brand characterised?** Being named as the expensive option is a
  mention and a problem at the same time.
- **Would this answer satisfy a buyer?** If yes and you are absent, you have a
  real gap. If the answer is poor for everyone, the category is open.

---

## Why two runs of the same question differ

Answer engines are not deterministic. The same scenario produces different
wording, and sometimes different brands, from one run to the next.

This is why a single conversation is evidence and a single conversation is not
proof. Read several before concluding anything, and treat
[trends](../dashboards/trend-tracking.html) rather than readings as the signal.

---

## When a result looks wrong

Check the scenario first. A large share of surprising results come from a
scenario that does not ask what you thought it asked — too broad, too narrow,
or phrased in a way no buyer would use.
