# Sentiment Analysis

Every mention of your brand in an answer is classified as positive, neutral or
negative, so you can see not only whether you appear but how you are described.

For how to read the numbers and act on them, see
[Sentiment Analysis](../insights/sentiment-analysis.html) under Insights. This
page covers how the classification works.

---

## What is classified

The mention, in its context — not the whole answer. An answer can be positive
about a competitor and neutral about you, and those are recorded separately.

---

## Why most mentions are neutral

A factual answer listing options is neutral by nature. An engine naming five
tools is not praising any of them.

A profile that is mostly neutral is normal and healthy. A profile that is
strongly positive usually means few mentions rather than glowing ones, and is
worth checking rather than celebrating.

---

## What moves it

Sentiment follows sources. Engines describe you using the language of what they
retrieved, so a shift usually means a new source entered the picture — a review,
a thread, a comparison.

When sentiment moves, the fastest explanation is in
[Most Cited Sources](../insights/most-cited-sources.html): compare what was
cited before and after.

---

## Its limits

Sentiment is a summary. It tells you the temperature, not the claim. To find
the specific thing being said — "expensive", "hard to set up" — use
[Perceptions](../core-concepts/perceptions.html), which can be tracked
individually over time.
