# Detecting Citations

When an answer engine supports a claim with a source, Genezio records it. Those
records are what make visibility explicable rather than merely measurable.

---

## Why citations are the useful half

A visibility score tells you where you stand. Citations tell you what produced
it — and a source is something you can act on, where a score is not.

If a competitor is recommended in thirty answers and the same comparison page
is cited in twenty of them, you do not have a thirty-answer problem. You have
one page to deal with.

---

## What gets captured

The source behind a claim, the answer it supported, and — where the source
identifies one — the channel or author who published it. See
[Channels and Authors](../insights/channels-and-authors.html).

Counting across conversations gives you
[Citation Frequency](../dashboards/citation-frequency.html): which sources
shape your category, ranked.

---

## Not every answer cites

Engines differ. Some cite heavily, some answer from what the model already
holds and cite nothing. An answer without citations is not a failure of
detection; it means the engine did not show its working.

Those answers still count for visibility. They are simply less diagnosable, and
a category answered largely without citations is one where your standing rests
on what models absorbed in training rather than on what they can retrieve now.

---

## What to do with them

The practical loop:

1. Find a topic where you lose.
2. Open the conversations and read what was cited.
3. Decide whether you can be a better source for that question than what is
   cited now.

That is a brief with a known audience and a known competitor, which is a
different thing from a content calendar.
