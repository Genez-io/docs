# Using Query Fanouts for Content

A [query fanout](../analysis/extracting-query-fanouts.html) is what an answer
engine actually searched for after somebody asked it a question. Writing
against fanouts means writing against what retrieval looks for, rather than
against the sentence a human typed.

---

## Why it changes the brief

A human question and the queries behind it are different texts. Somebody asks
"which CRM should I use for a small agency"; the engine goes looking for
pricing for small teams, integrations with design tools, limits on user seats.

A page written against the human question covers the subject. A page written
against the fanouts answers the five things the engine was actually trying to
find — and those are what it will match on.

---

## What to take from them

**The vocabulary.** Fanouts use the words an engine expects a good source to
contain. If your page says "seats" and the fanouts say "users", you are harder
to retrieve for no good reason.

**The sub-questions.** One buyer question frequently expands into several. Each
one is a section the piece should genuinely answer, not mention.

**The ones you nearly match.** Some fanouts your existing pages almost serve.
Those are cheaper wins than the ones you miss entirely — often an edit rather
than a new piece.

---

## In Content Hub

Fanouts can be carried straight into a brief, so the outline is built from them
rather than from an assumption about the topic. The writer then has a specific
list of questions to answer instead of a subject to cover.

See [Briefs](briefs.html) and
[From Data to Content Strategy](from-data-to-content-strategy.html).

---

## A caution

Do not write a page per fanout. Fanouts are fragments of one intent, and a page
built around a fragment is thin by construction. Group the fanouts that belong
to one real question, and write one substantial piece that answers all of them.
