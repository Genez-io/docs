# Covering Several Topics in One Piece

A brief or an article can cover more than one topic.

---

## Why you would

Topics in Genezio are narrow on purpose — that is what makes visibility
measurable. Real content is often wider. A single buyer's guide can legitimately
answer questions that sit under three separate topics, and splitting it into
three thin pages would serve nobody.

Selecting several topics tells Genezio to write one piece that earns across all
of them, rather than one piece per topic.

---

## When to do it

**Do** combine topics that a reader would expect in one place. A comparison
page naturally covers pricing, features and alternatives.

**Do not** combine topics just to save time. A piece stretched across unrelated
questions answers none of them well, and answer engines lift passages — a page
that is vaguely about four things has no passage worth lifting.

A good test: would a person searching for topic A be happy to land on this
page? If not, write two pieces.

---

## What changes in the result

The brief covers the questions from every topic you selected, and the article
is written to answer them in one structure. When you later look at which topics
the piece supports, it appears under each of them.
