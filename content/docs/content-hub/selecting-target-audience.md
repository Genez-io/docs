# Selecting Target Audience

Who a piece is for changes what it has to say, what it can assume, and which
words it uses. Content Hub asks because a generic audience produces generic
content that answers nobody's question precisely.

---

## Why it matters for AI visibility specifically

Answer engines lift passages that directly answer a question. A passage written
for "anyone interested in CRM" rarely answers anything precisely enough to be
worth lifting.

A passage written for a sales lead at a twenty-person agency answers a question
somebody actually asked — and that is the kind of passage that gets quoted.

---

## Choosing well

**Pick the person who asks the question**, not the person who signs the
contract. They are often different. The buyer's question and the user's
question pull different vocabulary and different sources.

**Use personas you already have.** If the brand has
[personas](../core-concepts/personas.html), they carry the context — role,
constraints, what they already know — and keep pieces consistent across a
team.

**Be specific about what they already know.** The single biggest difference
between a useful piece and a padded one is how much groundwork it skips.

---

## A common mistake

Choosing the widest audience to "reach more people". It produces a piece that
explains what everyone already knows and answers nothing specific. Narrow
pieces are more quotable, and more quotable means more cited.
