# Selecting Tone of Voice

Tone decides how the piece sounds. It does not decide whether it gets cited —
but a badly chosen tone can stop a good piece being useful.

---

## What to choose

Match the tone to where the reader is. Somebody comparing tools wants claims
they can check; somebody learning a concept wants patience.

The practical constraint for AI visibility: **tone must not get in the way of a
quotable passage**. Copy that builds for three sentences before making a point
is hard to lift. An engine looking for an answer wants a paragraph that
contains one.

---

## Tones that work against you

**Heavy marketing voice.** Superlatives and positioning language give an engine
nothing factual to quote, and independent sources will outrank you on the same
question.

**Excessive hedging.** A passage that qualifies every claim answers nothing
definitively, and definitive passages are what get cited.

**Clever framing.** Analogies and narrative openings delay the answer. Fine
for a newsletter, costly for a page meant to be retrieved.

---

## A simple test

Read the piece and ask: can I copy one paragraph that answers the question on
its own, without the paragraph before it?

If yes, the tone is working. If no, it is decoration, whatever it sounds like.

---

## Consistency

Keep tone consistent across a brand. Answer engines cite your pages alongside
each other, and a reader arriving from one to another notices when they sound
like different companies.
