# Generating Articles

Content Hub writes a full article from a brief, with the topic, the query
fanouts and the sources you selected already attached.

---

## Start from a brief, not a prompt

An article generated from a blank instruction is a generic article. One
generated from a [brief](briefs.html) is written against specific questions,
with specific sources, for a specific audience.

The brief is where the thinking goes. The article is the easy part.

---

## What you choose

**The topic or topics.** A piece can cover several where a reader would expect
them together — see
[Covering Several Topics](covering-several-topics-in-one-piece.html).

**The audience.** Who it is for changes the level, the assumptions and the
vocabulary. See [Selecting Target Audience](selecting-target-audience.html).

**The tone.** See [Selecting Tone of Voice](selecting-tone-of-voice.html).

**The sources.** Material the writer should draw on, including files you
attach. Each source is kept with the piece, so you can see later what it was
built from.

---

## Working in your own language

The article, and the conversation about it, are in your brand's language. See
[Working in Your Brand Language](working-in-your-brand-language.html).

---

## After it generates

Read it as an editor, not an approver. The useful question is not "is this
good" but "would an answer engine lift a passage from this to answer the
question we targeted". If no paragraph stands alone as an answer, it needs
work, however well it reads.

Then: [edit it](editing-articles.html), check its
[schema markup](schema-markup.html), and
[publish it](publishing-content.html).
