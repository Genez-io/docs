# Analyzing Landing Pages

A landing page is not an article, and judging it like one gives you the wrong
answer. An article is written to answer a question; a landing page is written
to sell. The Content Analyzer reads the two differently.

---

## What changes

An article is measured against a single scenario — one question, and how well
the piece answers it.

A landing page is measured against **every topic of your brand at once**, with
no single scenario. That matches how a landing page actually earns citations:
not by answering one question well, but by being findable across the whole
space of what buyers ask.

So the report leads with **Topic reach** — *can AI find this page for what it
asks?* — rather than fit to one prompt.

---

## What you get

**Topic reach.** How well a retriever finds this page across the questions AI
is asked about your topics, and the vocabulary of that demand.

**Quotable structure.** Whether the page is built for an answer engine to lift
a passage from. Marketing pages often fail here: strong claims, nothing
quotable.

**Copy vs. page furniture.** How much of the page is actual prose against
navigation, banners and boilerplate. A page that is 12% prose has little for a
model to stand on, however good that 12% is.

**What it could support.** A headline figure for how much of what AI already
writes about your topics this page could stand behind.

---

## Reading the result

The useful question is not "what score did it get" but "which of the three is
weak". They lead to different work:

- Weak **topic reach** is a content problem — the page is about something other
  than what buyers ask.
- Weak **quotable structure** is a formatting problem — the substance is there
  but nothing is liftable.
- Weak **copy vs. furniture** is a design problem — the page is mostly chrome.

---

## Pages the analyzer will not read

Some pages are not the kind this analysis can judge, and the analyzer stops
rather than returning a number that looks meaningful but is not. If you get
that result, the page is usually one with almost no prose at all.
